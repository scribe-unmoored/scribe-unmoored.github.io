Apocalyze

/əˈpɒk.ə.laɪz/

verb [transitive]; apocalyzes, apocalyzed, apocalyzing.

1. To imagine the instantaneous, spectacular, and disproportionately violent destruction of an annoying, obstructive, or inconvenient object, especially as a form of comedic exaggeration or emotional catharsis.

Usage: “I want to throw the Duolingo owl into the sun.”

An example of apocalyzing in which a minor source of irritation inspires an imagined act of extravagant and disproportionate destruction, expressed for humorous effect rather than as a genuine intention to cause harm.



Foiegrasiating

/ˌfwɑː.ɡrɑː.siˈeɪ.tɪŋ/

noun [uncountable]; also present participle of foiegrasiate, v.

1. The practice of elaborating upon the internal complexity of a seemingly simple argument until the rhetorical significance of its original premise is diminished by the weight of its broader implications.

2. By extension. A rhetorical technique whereby a narrow, exaggerated, or vaguely defined claim is contextualized within a substantially larger network of contributing factors, thereby reducing its apparent explanatory importance without necessarily disputing its validity.

Usage: “You're right, people are isolated... Through declining literacy rates, violence in and the underfunding of education, inadequate access to education, monetization and gentrification of public spaces, and yes, Dave, cell phone use.”

An example of foiegrasiating in which an argument attributing social isolation primarily to cell phone use is expanded to encompass numerous interconnected social problems, diminishing the rhetorical weight of the original explanation without explicitly rejecting it.

Lexical Note: While foiegrasiating can be a useful rhetorical tactic, it does not necessarily invalidate the original claim it is used against.



Fuckknuckling

/ˈfʌkˌnʌk.lɪŋ/

noun [uncountable]; also present participle of fuckknuckle, v.

1. The act or practice of striking an opponent's legs with the fists during physical combat, especially in a martial arts contest.

Usage: “DID YOU SEE THAT, JOE?! THE LEVEL CHANGE. HE FUCKKNUCKLED HIM, JOE! HE FUCKKNUCKLED HIM! IT'S UFC999 AND WE HAVE THE VERY FIRST TKO BY FUCKKNUCKLE!”

An example of fuckknuckling in competitive mixed martial arts, describing an unconventional striking technique in which a fighter deliberately targets an opponent's legs with punches.



Gretching

/ˈɡrɛtʃ.ɪŋ/

noun [uncountable]; also present participle of gretch, v.

1. The practice of invoking one's spiritual, personal, moral, or political beliefs to justify neglectful, harmful, or aggressive behavior, while simultaneously maintaining that positive conduct prescribed by those same beliefs is optional or nonobligatory.

2. By extension. The selective application of moral principles whereby obligations to refrain from wrongdoing are minimized or excused, while obligations to perform good deeds are treated as discretionary.

Usage: “You don't have to give to charity to be a good person. Besides, the world isn't fair, and handouts don't make anyone's life better.”

An example of gretching in which a professed moral framework is invoked to dismiss an obligation toward charitable conduct while invoking that neglecting those in need is a moral imperative, despite the fact that the speaker is unlikely to profess these precise beliefs.



Paroikeiophobia

/ˌpær.oʊˌkeɪ.oʊˈfoʊ.bi.ə/

noun [uncountable]

1. Fear of or aversion to unsolicited emotional intimacy or familiarity perceived as strategically motivated, particularly when such behavior is accepted as genuine by others.

2. By extension. Discomfort arising from the expectation to tolerate or reciprocate uninvited displays of emotional closeness, especially when no immediate practical or interpersonal purpose justifies such familiarity.

Usage: “The used car salesman came over, put his hand on my shoulder, called me his friend, and started talking about how much we had in common. Everyone else thought he was wonderful. I just wanted to crawl out of my skin.”

An example of paroikeiophobia in which a salesman's unsolicited display of personal familiarity provokes discomfort in its recipient, who perceives the interaction as strategically motivated despite its acceptance as genuine by others.
