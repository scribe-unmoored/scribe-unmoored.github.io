The Vindraum

When the Vindraum roars, there is no time left to run. Terrible beasts they are; bigger than a castle with an appetite to match. Wings that blot out the sun until you’re certain it’s turned night. But if you ever hear one roar, know it’s a terrible, wonderful sound—and that you’ll only ever hear it once.

They nest in the eastern range, halfway down the mountain slope where the cold can’t get to their eggs. A Vindraum can nest for nearly a century, and it takes twice as long for the young to be old enough to lay eggs of their own. They’re not ferocious. Not in the way some things are. A rabid dog will stagger down a street looking for a child to bite. A centaur will kill a man by arrow for amusement. A Vindraum will look on the most fragile creature you can imagine—something no more frightening than a newborn infant—and it’ll take to the skies like you’d just shown it the number of its days.

A Vindraum won’t fight you. Not that it couldn’t if it was forced. There’s a good reason why there’s no lords with ‘draum head over their hearths. The beasts are perfectly capable of defending themselves. A head full of spear-length teeth helps with that.

Let me ask you something. You ever walked through a cold forest when you come across a tree stripped of its leaves? I’m not talking about some red deer that’s come along and taken a few needles off the lower branches; I’m talking about evidence that something big’s come through and cleared the whole thing, branches and all, leaving what looks like a giant’s toothpick lodged in the ground. That’s a Vindraum. They’re not meat-eaters like your average dragon. They like plants—pine needles especially. They’ll clean a whole tree in a single bite. There ain’t no fighting something that big. Though that hasn’t stopped some fools from trying.

A Vindraum usually flies away at the first sign of danger. Unless of course, you go after their nest. Given the bastards take so long to breed, they’re especially protective of their young. They don’t abandon them when a threat comes around. If you’re lucky, they’ll just squash you or bite you in half.

Most dragons breathe fire. There’s the Heldraum what breathes ice, and the Sturmdraum what breathes lightning. You can ask many questions of the gods, and you’ll get sparse few answers in return. Why they gave the Vindraum its breath, I can’t tell you. If you send an army after one of the creatures, as some men have in the past, you’ll run the risk of pushing the beast past what its claws and teeth and tail can overcome.

Maybe the gods wanted to teach such men a lesson. Maybe they’d created the Vindraum as some sort of guardian and thought whatever could defeat it should have no chance to wreak havoc in the world. You see here, when a Vindraum issues its reluctant roar, that’s when you know it’s going to breathe its terrible sort of fire. That’s when you know, as I said, there’s no time left to run.

You ever seen the crater up at the northern pass? No trees. No walls. No hills for leagues in all directions. You can’t grow nothing there anymore neither. People come away with all sorts of sores and sickness when they try to get a look for themselves.

They say the day it happened, you could see it from everywhere. A great white light that made some go blind just from looking at it. So bright you couldn’t see the sun in the sky. Then a sound—a roar. Loud enough to drive a man deaf if he’d been within five leagues.

There was an omen that followed. An evil thing. Sign of the gods’ displeasure is what I say. A great cloud, taller than the mountains, they say. Shaped like a mushroom.
