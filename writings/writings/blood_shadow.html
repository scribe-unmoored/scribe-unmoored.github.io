The Blood Shadow

As the rains fell, we gathered about the firestone—a small thing, beating like a heart as its dying heat waxed and waned. Walks-Sideways busied himself distributing the fruits of our hunt: blue-fruit and a lizard-corpse. I watched him hide a morsel in his mouth in secret beforehand. But I would say nothing tonight. The young ones must eat, after all; and beside that, our hunt had been fruitful indeed. All mouths chewed in silence, the only sound to occupy our ears was the constant downpour outside. The perfect time for a story, I knew. Judging by the way that Walks-Sideways, Jumps-Good, and Blue-in-the-Eyes’ gazes drifted to me, they knew it too.

“Will you tell us something tonight, elder?” Jumps-Good was as his name suggested: perfectly capable of deductions, which he had the tendency to make rather rashly.

“I will, child. So long as you promise to be quiet this time.” The boy dipped his antlers in answer. A promising answer, to be sure. I settled onto my haunches. At my age, settling to the floor completely would take some time. Though I will tell you, I fully intended to arrive there. I took a deep breath, considering the tale most appropriate for tonight’s meal, when suddenly, our nascent peace was fractured.

“Flies-on-Vulture, he has taken an extra helping! I haven’t even finished my first!”

Ah, how foolish I was. Not all understand that sometimes peace is more valuable than justice—or that this was, in all reality, not much of an injustice in the first place. But such are the priorities of youth. I tossed my own morsel to Blue-in-the-Eyes, who was the one to bring such an unforgivable transgression to my attention.

“Is this true, Walks-Sideways?” I asked. His look of guilt betrayed him before his confession. “Was this hospitable of you?”

“No,” the boy answered timidly.

“And would you have liked it if your sister had done the same?”

“No, elder.”

“Then why have you done such a thing?”

“Because, I am hungry.”

“I rendered that, given your actions.” I let the boy soak for a moment. That would be enough to satiate his sister.

“He will still be hungry after this food is gone, elder!” Jumps-Good was quick to add his observation. “He eats more and more every day! Gate-mother says he will grow to be twice the size of our chieftain one day!”

“Oh?” I retrieved another morsel from the lizard-corpse. Perhaps the tale tonight would not be my own.

“He wanted to spear a white spirit, but I held him back, elder. He said he knew our food was not enough for him.” Blue-in-the-Eyes had a tendency to seek out trouble for her brother, when he was not seeking it out himself. But somehow I did not doubt the allegations, even coming from her. Regardless of their truth, it was a serious thing. And the perfect opportunity to settle the youths with a tale.

“A white spirit, you say?” I turned to the boy, who was looking up at me with great, supplicating eyes. It was a tale I’d told him before, when he wasn’t quite so big. Now that he was nearly my height and more, it was clear the tale he’d heard in his youth had not dulled. Perhaps it was time the same lesson be handed to the youngest of our tribe. The rains would grant us the time.

“Blue-in-the-Eyes, has your brother told you the story of why we do not hunt the white spirits?”

In answer, she dangled her antlers. Good, it was my tale to spoil, not the youth’s.

“Then sit, young ones. Settle down, and I will tell you the tale of the Blood Shadow.”

Almost at once, they laid on the floor, curling their legs beneath them. I took that as meaning they were ready to listen. At last.

“In the time before my father’s father, the world was different. The rains were lighter, and the world softer in some aspects, harder in others. A hunter in those days went into the jungles, in search of meat to feed his wife who was with child. After spending long cycles hiding from the rains and evading monsters for which our people have no names, he came upon the children of the white spirits. His need was great by then. He was starving after many days in the jungle alone, and he thought to his wife, who was alone and hungry. So he took the white spirits as prey, killing them.”

Blue-in-the-Eyes gasped, though she could not understand why this mistake had been so grave.

“The white spirits are connected to the cycles of the rains, and when one is killed, a dark revenant is summoned to avenge them. The Blood Shadow. It comes after the rain, dark with the blood of the slain, to right the balance of the world. In those days, they did not know these things, and so the hunter was killed before he could return to his family.”

Jumps-Good sat bolt upright, as he so often did when he had an observation to contribute. “Is that why we do not hunt the white spirits?”

“No, child. That is not why. At least, not entirely.” By now, I was settled against the ground, the warmth of the machines around us glowing into my flank. It brought me an ease that my bones had been missing in the days since our hunt began. How I relished a return to our stronghold! But teaching the hunt to these little ones was an important task, and I would teach it to them not only in action, but in spirit as well. “The Blood Shadow is blind, and does not know who killed the pups of its brethren. It will exact revenge against any it finds, until its rage is sated. Do you know of the Great Antlers of Rules-in-Red?” My question was unimportant. Each of the youths knew the monument, built high atop the Great City. Only their recognition of it mattered to me. Each dipped their antlers in answer.

“He was slain by the Blood Shadow, before a great age of fear and scarcity took hold of our people. In my youth, the jungles we traverse were not so plentiful as they are now. The Blood Shadow did not take only our leader, but also the balance of the very land.”

Jumps-Good was quick once again. “But you said the Blood Shadow brings balance?”

I dipped my antlers this time. He was a perceptive one. I tried to encourage that in him, while tempering his impatience where I could.

“Yes, young one; but I have told you already: The Blood Shadow is also blind.”
