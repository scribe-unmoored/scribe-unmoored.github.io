The Laws of Biology: A Retrospective Look at an Inadequate Understanding

Uriah Thomas, PhD; Jane Augustine, PhD; Cpt. Mark Adebayo, MS

Universal laws governing the study of biology were considered for the longest time to be impossible to formulate. The variables associated with any biological system are so complex, it was long believed that no single relationship could be reliably isolated and universally applied.

However, in the 2050s an intrepid young scientist by the name of Tona Dhingra formulated her “five laws of biology,” an exercise in recognizing the universal principles which govern all life. While we understand these axioms to be rather reductive in the modern day, they were nonetheless an audacious and earnest attempt at establishing a unifying organismal framework.

Dhingra’s First Law is simple, almost mathematically so:

The Law of Monophyly states that no organism, no matter how derived, ceases to inherit its ancestry. Any seeming reversion to some previous evolutionary form occurs secondarily. In her formulation: “You cannot grow out of your ancestry.”
